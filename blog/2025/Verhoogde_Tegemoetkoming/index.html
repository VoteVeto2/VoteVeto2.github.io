0. Introduction

For an international student study in Leuven, Vlaams, Belgium. Getting insured in Belgium does not goes so smooth like how you would pay for you meal - it would take you month, even a quarter until everything is set and you get your money back. The good news is, Belgium is considered one of the most low-cost countries in terms of health care and the service they get. The bad news is, it’s quite tricky and goes through lots of burecracy process that would just keep you waiting. And in case if you are not insured w/ anything, you would probably not go bankrupted like the US styple, but it would definitely makes you wallets much thinner.

So this is largely based on my personal experience, after spending nearly thousand euros in wrongful health insurance companies and uninsured Emergency Room/GP visit/pills, I come these as my expereiences. We need to talk about what I believe probably the most important part in Belgium Welfare System:

Verhoogde Tegemoetkoming

Below are guidance mainly summarised and generated by GPT-5-pro via web research, and I made modifications based on its output.

What is “Verhoogde Tegemoetkoming” (VT)?

Belgium’s “you-earn-less-so-healthcare-costs-you-less” status. If you have VT, doctor visits, meds and hospital stays are way cheaper - in gerenal speaking, it will be ~70% less than typical insurance and ~95% less than uninsured case; you only pay the copay (e.g., visit GP is €0-1). Think of it as a discount card issued by your health fund (mutualiteit).

Of course there’s a limit cap for the “low-income-person”, but if you are a student status not working full time in the same time, you will probably not reach that threadshold so don’t worry to much on it.

A pretty nice video intro on how to get it can be found in Youtbe, credit to @Amal

1. What VT changes within the Health Insurance

We will only cover the case w/ CM insurance(cuz that’s the one I am insured with and the only company that I know about XD)

GP consultations (geconventioneerde GP)

  • You pay only the small co‑pay (remgeld); the rest is billed to the fund via the third‑party payer. VT patients have a right to this at the GP. (RIZIV)

  • Examples:

    • GP consult with GMD (globaal medisch dossier): €1.00 remgeld with VT (vs €4.00 without VT).
    • GP consult without GMD: €1.50 remgeld with VT (vs €6.00 without VT). (CM)

Psychological care (two layers)

  • Publicly funded first‑line psychology (RIZIV networks): for adults ≥24 y the personal share is €4 per session with VT (vs €11). See CM’s explanation of network‑based care. (CM)
  • CM’s extra member benefit (outside the network): CM refunds part of sessions with recognised clinicians; for children & youth CM states 50% up to €30 per session, or 75% up to €45 per session if you have VT (up to lifetime caps). (CM)

Medicines & contraception

  • VT leads to higher reimbursement across many reimbursable medicines (lower remgeld). RIZIV confirms an extra reimbursement for contraceptives for people ≤24 years or with VT regardless of age/gender (morning‑after pill is reimbursed for everyone). (RIZIV)

Hospitalisation: lower personal shares + CM top‑ups for VT

  • RIZIV confirms that a patient’s personal share (co‑pay) depends on status, and VT reduces what you pay. (RIZIV)
  • CM details the fixed hospital forfaits where VT pays less or nothing; e.g., forfait medical imaging co‑pay is €1.98 with VT (vs €6.20), forfait technical procedures per admission is €0 with VT, and per‑day hospital medicines forfait is €0 with VT. (CM)
  • CM extra support if you have VT and no hospital insurance: CM pays 75% of the legally defined personal shares (room, medical/paramedical fees, and top‑ups on reimbursable medicines). This is a CM‑specific complementary benefit. (CM)

Maximumfactuur (MAF): yearly cap on copay

  • Belgium’s Maximumfactuur(Maximum Amount) caps a family’s annual co‑pays; with VT you benefit from the “social MAF” at a lower cap. For 2025, social MAF is €527.27; if a chronic‑care trigger is met, the cap drops to €410.10 (HZIV)
  • General info pages on MAF from Vlaanderen.be and CM explain how caps apply and that VT households can be treated as a separate “VT‑sub‑family” for the social MAF. (Vlaanderen.be)

2. Other deductions on tickets, fees etc.

Public transport (student‑relevant)

  • De Lijn (Flanders): with VT you are entitled to a reduced‑rate annual pass (Buzzy Pazz 6–24y or Omnipas 25–64y). Discount is applied automatically when buying/renewing online or at a point of sale (they check your VT via the national register). De Lijn’s pages list the VT discount and automatic processing. (De Lijn)
  • NMBS/SNCB (trains): VT holders get 50% off standard tickets with the VT discount (via a specific entitlement loaded and checked at purchase). See NMBS’s discount overview and the “Large families, persons …” page on how the VT entitlement is verified. (NMBS announced a new fare structure earlier in 2025, but as of 30 Sep 2025 the official discount page still lists 50% for VT holders—check at purchase in case of updates.) (Belgian Train)
  • MIVB/STIB (Brussels): VT holders can buy the RVV pass at a reduced price—€8.10 per month or €85 per year (2025 page). ([STIB - MIVB][16])

Flemish “zorgpremie” (care premium) of the Vlaamse Sociale Bescherming

  • If you had VT on 1 Jan of the previous year, you pay the reduced zorgpremie. In 2025 the standard premium is €64; with VT it’s €32. (The Flemish government has indicated a general increase from 2026; but 2025 values are as stated here.) ([Departement Zorg][17])

Leuven‑specific municipal benefits connected to VT

  • Free UiTPAS (kansentarief) + 80% discount on partner culture/sport/leisure activities and membership/registration fees across Leuven, Bierbeek, Herent, Oud‑Heverlee. The city explicitly lists this under VT. ([Homepage Stad Leuven][18])
  • Free annual package of household waste bags if you have VT (city measure; see the Leuven page for details and the 2025 council decision). ([Homepage Stad Leuven][19])
  • The Leuven VT page also points to De Lijn/NMBS VT transport discounts, local support (e.g., energy coaching) and “Iedereen Verdient Vakantie” discounted trips. ([Homepage Stad Leuven][18])

Telecom/internet (important nuance)

  • Since 1 Mar 2024, Belgium runs a new “Sociaal internetaanbod”. Having VT by itself does not make you eligible (eligibility is based on other social criteria); official guidance is explicit on this point. ([Economie][20])

3. Special offers & KUL-student‑oriented extras

UiTPAS Leuven kansentarief

  • With a recent attest VT, Leuven grants a free UiTPAS and 80% discounts on tickets, membership and course fees at participating organisations in Leuven/Bierbeek/Herent/Oud‑Heverlee; great for cinema, community sports, cultural venues, workshops. See examples and application steps (accepts a VT attest not older than 3 months; annual auto‑renew where still eligible). ([Homepage Stad Leuven][21])

KU Leuven Sport – (partial) reimbursement via your mutuality

  • CM’s sports‑club benefit: up to €15 per person per year for membership at a sports club, sports centre/service, recognised jogging or swimming initiations, etc. You can apply this to (part of) your KU Leuven Sports Card or fitness subscription. ([CM][22])
  • KU Leuven Sports publishes the mutuality forms and explains the one‑time yearly reimbursement process (get the right CM form, have KU Leuven Sport stamp it, and submit to CM). ([KU Leuven][23])

CM student travel assistance for study abroad (Erasmus, etc.)

  • If you’re studying in another European country, CM’s student travel assistance gives access to the Mutas emergency centre and reimbursement for urgent medical costs abroad (complements your European Health Insurance Card). Notify CM before you go. ([CM][24])

A few other CM extras students often use

  • Vaccinations: certain vaccines are reimbursed by the compulsory insurance and CM; CM also documents travel‑vaccine reimbursements pathways (and via optional CM MediKo Plan). ([CM][25])
  • Eye care: if there’s no statutory reimbursement, CM pays €40/year towards a new pair of glasses as a member benefit (separate from optional insurances). ([CM][26])

Quick how‑to (claiming the main benefits)

  1. Check/obtain proof of VT In Mijn CM or request an Attest verhoogde tegemoetkoming (CM forms). You’ll need this for UiTPAS, sometimes for transport products. (CM)

  2. Use the third‑party payer at the GP As a VT patient, your GP must apply it; you pay only the remgeld (e.g., €1 with GMD). (RIZIV)

  3. Psychological care Prefer RIZIV network psychologists when possible (co‑pay €4 with VT), and use CM’s extra refunds where applicable (youth: up to 75%/€45 with VT). (CM)

  4. Public transport

    • De Lijn: buy or renew your Buzzy Pazz/Omnipas; the VT discount is applied automatically online once your national number is entered. ([help.delijn.be][27])
    • NMBS: ensure your VT entitlement is recognised at a ticket office or in your online profile to get 50% discount on tickets. (Belgian Train)
    • MIVB: buy the RVV subscription (listed price: €8.10/month or €85/year). ([STIB - MIVB][16])

  5. UiTPAS (Leuven region) Apply online or at a sales point with your VT attest (<3 months old) to get a free card and 80% discounts at partner organisations. ([Homepage | Stad Leuven][21])

  6. KU Leuven Sports Card reimbursement Download CM’s sports reimbursement form, get it stamped at KU Leuven Sport, and submit to CM (up to €15/year). ([CM][22])

  7. Keep an eye on your annual cap If your remgeld reaches the MAF cap (with VT usually €527.27 in 2025), the rest of that year’s eligible co‑pays are reimbursed automatically. (HZIV)

Notes, limits & recent changes to be aware of

  • Hospitalisation billing: choose common or two‑bed rooms and conventioned providers to avoid supplements; CM’s VT hospital top‑up (75% of personal shares if you lack hospital insurance) is exceptional but only covers legally defined personal shares. (CM)
  • NMBS discount rounding / tariff changes: NMBS announced a new fare structure in 2025; at the time of writing, the official page still shows 50% VT discount—verify at purchase in case of roll‑out changes. (Belgian Train)
  • Telecom: VT alone does not grant the new social internet offer; eligibility depends on other social criteria. ([Economie][20])
  • Flemish care premium: 2025 amounts are €64 standard / €32 with VT; Flemish sources indicate increases are planned from 2026—monitor when budgeting future years. ([Departement Zorg][17])
