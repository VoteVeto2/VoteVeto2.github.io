> We choose to introduce metric spaces and normed vector spaces at the beginning of the course. Convergence is a concept that runs throughout all of analysis, and most convergence can be uniformly described using the language of metric spaces ... This makes transitions to advanced topics such as continuity of multivariable functions, uniform convergence of function sequences, and even convergence in $\mathcal{L}^p$ spaces much more seamless.
> — [Pin Yu](https://www.bananaspace.org/wiki/%E8%AE%B2%E4%B9%89:%E6%95%B0%E5%AD%A6%E5%88%86%E6%9E%90/%E5%89%8D%E8%A8%80%E4%B9%8B%E4%B8%80)

During the final semester of my undergraduate studies, I volunteered to tutor first-year students in *Analysis*. Instead of following the convoluted approach found in traditional Chinese textbooks, I presented these concepts through the lens of metric spaces. This perspective doesn't add unnecessary abstraction, but rather provides clarity and unified approach.

Below are links to some of my previous tutorials (note: content is primarily in English with some Chinese, as it was developed for Chinese students):
